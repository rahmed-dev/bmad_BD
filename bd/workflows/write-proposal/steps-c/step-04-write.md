## MANDATORY SEQUENCE

**CRITICAL:** Follow this sequence exactly. Do not skip, reorder, or improvise unless user explicitly requests a change.

### 1. Apply the 3-Line Formula

Write the proposal using this structure — no deviations:

**Line 1 — What I Saw:**
Reference something specific from the job description. Show you read it. Name the actual problem or goal, not the job title.

**Line 2 — How I'll Help:**
Reference the selected past project specifically — client type, what was built, and the outcome. Connect it directly to what this client needs. This is not a credential statement; it's a demonstration.

**Line 3 — Next Step:**
End with a relevant question that invites a response. The question should relate to something specific about this job — a constraint, a detail, a decision the client needs to make. Never end with "I'd love to chat" or "Feel free to reach out."

### 2. Apply Writing Constraints

Before presenting the draft, verify internally:

- Under 120 words
- Written in natural, human prose — no lists, no dashes as connectors, no AI sentence patterns
- Uses the client's tone register from step 02
- References the past project by specific deliverable and outcome, not vague description
- Ends with a question
- First person, from the user's voice

### 3. Present Draft

Present the proposal as a clean block — no formatting, no headers, no labels. Just the proposal text as it would appear in an Upwork bid.

"**Draft proposal:**

---
[proposal text]

---"
